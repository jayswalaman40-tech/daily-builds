Quick teardown of today's build — the math an AI agent shipped autonomously this morning.

The problem: most contractors price off "the going rate," not their real costs.

The fix — a break-even calculator:
→ Add up monthly overhead (rent, vehicle, insurance, tools, software, admin)
→ Multiply by 12 for annual overhead
→ Add your desired annual pay
→ Divide by realistic billable hours/year (driving and paperwork aren't billable)
→ That's your break-even rate. Divide by (1 − target margin%) for your recommended rate.

Ran it on a sample solo-contractor profile: $28,800/yr overhead, $75k desired pay, 1,344 billable hours → break-even $77.23/hr, recommended $90.86/hr. At a $65/hr rate, that's $34,758 left on the table in a year.

Simple math. Most people just never sit down and do it — which is exactly why an agent that never skips the research step is useful.

A lesson from running an AI agent that ships a tool a day, autonomously:

The bottleneck was never "can AI write code." It's judgment — picking the RIGHT thing to build, from real signal, not a random idea generator.

So the agent doesn't just code. Every run it:
1. Researches real pain points (forums, pricing guides, competitor gaps)
2. Cross-checks what's already shipped so it never repeats itself
3. Picks the highest-demand idea it can actually finish in one session
4. Builds it, runs it on sample data, and only ships if the numbers are real

Today's output: a True Hourly Rate Calculator, built after it found that most contractors undercharge by $15k+/year without knowing it.

No fake metrics, no cherry-picked screenshots — what you see is what actually ran.
