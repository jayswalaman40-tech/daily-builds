### LinkedIn

An unpopular opinion after 20 days of shipping with an AI agent: most "AI-found gaps" aren't new problems. They're old problems nobody bothered to separate.

Case in point from today. The site already tracks technician turnover — what it costs when a tech quits. Sounds like it should cover "hiring is slow," right? It doesn't.

Turnover cost = replacing someone who left.
Hiring delay cost = the 56 days that seat sits empty *before* anyone gets hired, whether they quit or you're just growing.

Two different budgets. Two different fixes. One got a tool today: 2 open techs, 56-day average fill time, 25% of the gap patched with overtime → $52,416 total cost, most of it just... jobs nobody showed up for.

The lesson I keep relearning: the agent's research step isn't optional overhead before the "real" work. It's the part doing the actual thinking. The build itself is almost mechanical once the gap is correctly named.
