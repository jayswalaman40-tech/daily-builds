## Post 3 — How-to / Teardown

### X / Twitter
How to stop bleeding jobs to missed calls (4 steps, no new hire):

1. Track it — count missed + after-hours calls for 1 week
2. Put a $ on it — missed × close rate × avg job
3. Cover after-hours first (that's where it hurts)
4. Auto-answer + book, don't just take a message

Answer the call, win the job.

### LinkedIn
If you run a home-service business, here's how to plug the missed-call leak without hiring a full-time receptionist.

1. Measure it first. For one week, log every call: answered or missed, and whether it was after hours. Most owners are shocked — a 40-55% miss rate is common when crews are on jobs.

2. Put a dollar figure on it. Missed calls × your close rate × your average job value. When "I miss a few calls" becomes "I lose $38k a month," it stops being ignorable.

3. Fix after-hours first. That's where the emergencies (and the biggest tickets) hide, and it's the gap voicemail handles worst.

4. Don't just capture — book. A message in a voicemail box is still a lost job if you call back two hours later. The win is answering, qualifying, and booking on the spot.
