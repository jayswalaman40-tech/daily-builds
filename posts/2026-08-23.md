## Post 2 — How-to / Teardown

### X / Twitter
How to clean a bought lead list in 4 steps (before you waste a single email):

1. Validate the email format → kill the fakes
2. Lowercase + trim everything
3. Dedupe by email, not by name
4. Normalize phone numbers to one format

Skip step 3 and you'll email the same person 3x.

### LinkedIn
If you buy or export lead lists, here's the exact cleaning order I use before a single cold email goes out. Do it in this sequence and you avoid the two mistakes that get agencies flagged as spam.

1. Validate email format first. If it doesn't match a real pattern, drop it. Invalid emails = bounces = your domain reputation tanks.

2. Normalize the text. Trim whitespace, lowercase emails, fix name casing. "JOHN  SMITH " and "john smith" should look identical to your system.

3. Deduplicate by email, not by name. Two people can share a name; an email is unique. Dedupe by name and you'll delete real leads — or worse, email the same person three times.

4. Normalize phone numbers to one format (digits + a leading +). Your CRM and dialer will thank you.

I built a small script that does all four automatically. On a sample list it removed 50% as junk in seconds. Happy to share how it works — comment "clean" and I'll walk you through it.

## Post 3 — Opinion / Lesson

### X / Twitter
Unpopular take: your cold outreach isn't broken. Your list is.

You can write the perfect email and still lose if 50% of the addresses bounce or repeat.

Fix the list first. The copy matters second.

### LinkedIn
Everyone obsesses over cold-email copy. Almost nobody checks the list first — and that's backwards.

I cleaned a sample lead list today and 50% of it was junk: broken emails, duplicates, inconsistent formatting. If you send to a list like that, here's what happens before anyone even reads your message:

→ Bounces pile up
→ Your domain reputation drops
→ Even your good emails start landing in spam

The best copywriter in the world can't out-write a dirty list.

So the lesson I'm taking into this week: fix the inputs before you polish the output. A clean list of 500 beats a messy list of 5,000.
