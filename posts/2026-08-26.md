## Post 2 — How-to / Teardown

### X / Twitter
How I score a booking for no-show risk (the model behind today's tool):

1. Start at a base risk of 10
2. <6h notice → +35, <24h → +20, <48h → +8
3. Not confirmed → +25
4. Prior no-shows → +12 each (capped at 3)
5. Fri/Sat booking → +8

Score ≥60 = High. That's it.

### LinkedIn
Here's the teardown of the scoring model behind today's build, the No-Show Predictor.

No-show risk isn't random. It's a handful of signals you already have sitting in your booking system:

1. **Notice window** — under 6 hours adds +35 points, under 24h adds +20, under 48h adds +8. Last-minute bookings are the least sticky.
2. **Confirmation status** — no confirmation reply adds +25. Silence is the single strongest signal in the model.
3. **No-show history** — every prior no-show from that customer adds +12 points, capped at 3 instances (+36 max). Past behavior predicts future behavior.
4. **Day of week** — Friday and Saturday bookings get a small +8 bump. Weekend plans compete with your appointment.

Every job starts at a base risk of 10 and stacks these factors. 60+ = High risk. 35-59 = Medium. Under 35 = Low.

Ran on a sample week of 20 bookings, this simple model flagged 10 jobs (50%) as High risk — and they accounted for $4,770, or 63%, of the week's booked revenue.

No ML, no black box. Just the signals you already have, weighted and added up.

## Post 3 — Opinion / Lesson

### X / Twitter
Unpopular opinion: "no-shows are random" is an excuse.

In my test batch, every High-risk job shared the same 3 traits: short notice, no confirmation, repeat history.

Predictable ≠ unavoidable. You just have to look.

### LinkedIn
A lesson from building today's tool: no-shows feel random until you actually look at the data behind them.

When I ran a sample week of bookings through the No-Show Predictor I built today, the "risky" jobs weren't scattered evenly. They clustered hard around three traits: booked with less than 6 hours' notice, never confirmed, and a customer with a prior no-show on record.

Half the week's bookings hit that profile. And that half represented 63% of the week's revenue — not because those jobs were bigger, but because there were simply more of them sitting unconfirmed.

The uncomfortable part: none of this requires new tech to fix. A same-day confirmation text on every High-risk booking would catch most of it. Most businesses just don't have a way to tell which bookings are High risk until the truck is already parked outside an empty house.

This is the same insight behind AnswerCatch, honestly — a missed call is a missed job, and an unconfirmed booking is a job about to become one. Both are fixable before they cost you anything, if something is actually watching for it.
