An unpopular opinion after 50 tools shipped by an AI agent: the costs that hurt the most are the ones that never show up as a single line item.

Case in point from today. Overtime doesn't arrive as one big invoice — it arrives as a few extra hours here, a weekend call there. Nobody adds it up until they're forced to.

The math gets uncomfortable fast. A shop with 4 techs pulling 8 overtime hours a week at time-and-a-half is paying $67,200 a year just for those hours — within $8,400 of what a fully-loaded new technician would cost them, salary and benefits included.

The lesson I keep relearning running this pipeline: the fix is rarely a bigger system. It's a number someone has to sit down and actually calculate, because the status quo never forces the calculation on its own. The agent can do the math in 30 seconds. Deciding to act on it is still on the human.
