## Post 2 — Opinion / Lesson

### X / Twitter
Unpopular opinion: your flat-rate price sheet is probably 2 years out of date.

Labor and material costs went up. Your "quick job" prices didn't.

In my test batch, the small fast jobs were the least profitable — not the big installs.

Check your numbers, not your gut.

### LinkedIn
Here's a lesson from building today's tool: the jobs you assume are profitable are often the ones bleeding the most margin.

When I ran a sample quote log through the Quote & Margin Checker I built today, the big-ticket jobs — water heaters, mini-splits, panel upgrades — all cleared a healthy 40%+ margin.

It was the small, fast jobs — drain cleaning, a faucet install, a thermostat swap — that were running at 2-20% margin. Barely breaking even once you count drive time and dispatch cost.

Why? Those are usually the jobs still priced off an old flat-rate card. Nobody re-checks the math on a "quick job" because it feels too small to matter. But run 10-15 of those a month and the gap adds up to real money.

The fix isn't complicated: know your true cost (labor + materials + trip), pick a target margin, and quote from that number — not from memory.

Built a free calculator that does exactly that: https://answercatch.com/tools/quote-margin-checker/

## Post 3 — Results / Case Study

### X / Twitter
Ran a 20-quote sample log through today's build.

Average margin looked fine: 41.5%.
But 30% of the quotes were quietly under 20% margin.

An average hides a leak. Check the worst quotes, not just the mean.

### LinkedIn
A number from today's test run that's easy to miss if you only look at averages.

I fed a sample log of 20 job quotes into the tool I built today. The average margin across all 20 jobs: 41.5%. Looks healthy.

But look job-by-job and a different picture shows up: 6 of the 20 quotes (30%) were priced below a 30% target margin — one as low as 1.7%. Those 6 jobs alone left $201 on the table in this single batch.

The average was propped up by a few big installs with fat margins. It hid the fact that nearly a third of the jobs were quietly unprofitable.

If you only ever check your average margin at the end of the month, you'll miss exactly this — the quiet leak sitting inside your "quick jobs." Check the distribution, not just the mean.
